# scvrd-consumer-services-policies

The reader is a person with a disability in South Carolina (or someone
helping them) who wants to use SCVRD vocational rehabilitation (VR) services
and understand the rules. SCVRD publishes its rules as the **Consumer
Services Policies (CSP)** manual. Every factual claim this skill makes must
trace to a source in [references/verified-sources.md](references/verified-sources.md),
and **no source other than SCVRD** is used as original documentation.

## What SCVRD is and how the manual works

SCVRD is the state agency that helps people with disabilities prepare for,
get, keep, or advance in a job. Its policies (CSP manual, rev. 07-2025) set
the rules for every service. Per CSP 1.10, services are based on the
consumer's rehabilitation needs as written in their **Individualized Plan
for Employment (IPE)**, and the consumer exercises **informed choice**
throughout the whole process. "Informed choice" means the consumer gets
counseling and guidance about the options and decides with the counselor —
not for them.

## The VR process, step by step

Use [references/consumer-process-checklist.md](references/consumer-process-checklist.md)
for the consumer-facing checklist. The policy backbone:

1. **Referral and application.** The person applies for VR services and a
   counselor is assigned.
2. **Eligibility determination** (CSP 2.20). The counselor determines whether
   the person is eligible and sets their priority for services. Medical
   evaluations may be used at this stage only as needed to determine
   eligibility or priority (per CSP 4.40).
3. **IPE development** (CSP 2.40). The counselor and consumer jointly write
   the Individualized Plan for Employment. The IPE must:
   - name a vocational (job) goal chosen by the consumer;
   - list the services needed to reach that goal, with start dates and
     whether SCVRD provides or purchases each service;
   - include Vocational Rehabilitation Counseling and Guidance as a service.
   - It may **not** name an uncompensated outcome (for example, homemaker
     or unpaid family worker), and it may not name a job goal that needs a
     license the consumer cannot qualify for (for example, because of past
     convictions) unless a full vocational assessment supports it.
4. **Services.** The services on the IPE are delivered. The service menu is
   CSP Section 4 — see the topic lookup below.
5. **Case closure and post-employment.** After the consumer becomes employed,
   SCVRD can provide **post-employment services** (CSP 2.50) — but only before
   the case is closed, only through an IPE amendment, and only when the need
   is limited in scope and duration. Once a person exits and is closed, they
   must reapply and be found eligible again to get services.

## Money: who pays for what

Two policies control the money side. Explain both whenever a service costs
SCVRD money.

### Financial need test (CSP 3.10)

- SCVRD checks the household's financial picture with form **SCVR 4b** and
  may ask the consumer to share in the cost of **non-exempt** services.
- **No financial test** for: eligibility/vocational-needs assessments,
  counseling and guidance, referral and information services, job-related
  services (job search, placement, retention, follow-up), personal assistance
  services, rehabilitation technology consultations, post-employment services
  made up of the exempt services, Pre-Employment Transition Services for
  students (Pre-ETS), transportation tied to eligibility assessments or
  Pre-ETS, and auxiliary aids/504/ADA services (interpreters, readers).
- The consumer is also exempt when they (or their household) get **SSI,
  SSDI, SNAP, or TANF**, or household income is at or below **300% of the
  Federal Poverty Guidelines**.
- If the person refuses to share financial information or sign the form,
  they owe 100% of the non-exempt services.
- The analysis is redone at least yearly and whenever money circumstances
  change.

### Comparable services and benefits (CSP 3.20)

- Before SCVRD pays, the consumer must apply for and use other available
  programs that could cover the service (for example, Medicaid, a school
  program, or another agency) — SCVRD pays to avoid an interruption or delay
  but the consumer keeps pursuing the other benefit.
- Comparable-benefits rules do **not** apply to: assessments, counseling
  and guidance (including information and referral), job-related services,
  rehabilitation technology, and post-employment services.

## Rights and complaints

CSP 1.20 is the rights policy. If a consumer disagrees with a decision —
including a decision about funding services (see CSP 3.10 §3.6.C) — the
chain is: **Area Supervisor review → the Client Assistance Program (CAP)
→ mediation and/or an impartial hearing**. Tell the reader these options
exist early, before they need them.
